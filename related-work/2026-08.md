# Related work - August 2026

## 2026-08-09

- **Beyond Native Norms: Decolonizing AI Speech Recognition for Accent Equity in Nigerian ESL Classrooms** — type: paper · Center of Artificial Intelligence · 2026-08-07 · tags: [asr, eval] · languages: [Nigerian English]
  Synthesises the second-language speech and World Englishes literature to argue that commercial ASR treats educated Nigerian English as defective rather than as a legitimate variety, citing 20% to over 40% higher error rates on non-native, regional, and Outer Circle Englishes than on Inner Circle benchmarks. The proposal is pedagogical rather than technical: an "eco-contextual" model in which ASR feedback is mediated by a teacher and validated sociolinguistically instead of being taken as ground truth about pronunciation. No system is built or measured here and the error-rate figures come from prior work, so the operative contribution is the argument that open African-accented speech corpora are the prerequisite for fixing the underlying bias.
  *Igbo relevance:* Igbo-accented Nigerian English is the code-switching partner in most naturally occurring Igbo speech, so an ASR system that penalises it will also fail on the English spans inside otherwise Igbo recordings; the paper is most useful as a framing for why per-variety error reporting and locally recorded evaluation audio matter more than a headline WER. <https://doi.org/10.65591/cai-206-2026>

- **An NLP Tool for Translating Welfare Information: A Hybrid Controlled-Language and Domain-Adapted NMT Approach** — type: paper · CRC Press (book chapter) · 2026-08-07 · tags: [mt, method, eval] · languages: [unspecified low-resource]
  Describes a deployed pipeline for rendering legalistic public-benefits documents into plain target-language text, combining enforcement of a controlled source register, domain-adapted NMT, rule-based validation of the numerals and eligibility conditions that carry the most risk, and human review on high-stakes passages. Evaluation is task-based rather than BLEU-based, scoring whether users could complete a benefits task and how often high-risk errors survived, with reported gains over off-the-shelf MT on both. The chapter is thin on specifics (no language inventory, base model, or dataset sizes appear in the abstract), so it is logged for the pipeline design and evaluation framing rather than for reproducible numbers.
  *Igbo relevance:* constraining the source register before translating and then validating the output against rules is a cheap route to Igbo MT that is safe enough for government and health communication long before general-purpose quality arrives, and a task-completion metric fits Igbo better than BLEU, where references are scarce and orthographic disagreement depresses surface-overlap scores independently of meaning. <https://doi.org/10.1201/9781042009053-36>

- **Automatic transformation of Kazakh text into sign language glosses using multilingual transformer-based models** — type: paper · Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence · 2026-08-07 · tags: [mt, method] · languages: [Kazakh]
  Builds an 11,190-pair Kazakh text-to-gloss dataset from educational material and fine-tunes mT5-small and mT5-base on it, reporting that the larger model gains roughly 2.6 BLEU (87.5 to 90.1) along with better morphological handling and token coverage. The absolute scores are high enough to suggest the gloss mapping is close to deterministic, so the headline numbers say more about the task than the method; what is worth having is the controlled small-versus-base comparison at about ten thousand pairs, which is the data regime most Igbo sequence-to-sequence work sits in.
  *Igbo relevance:* a borderline keep, retained for the data-regime finding rather than the language or the task, namely that at roughly ten thousand pairs capacity rather than data is still the binding constraint, which argues against defaulting to the smallest multilingual checkpoint when fine-tuning Igbo MT; the morphological error analysis is a secondary draw given Igbo's agglutinative verb complexes. <https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1867966>

## 2026-08-08

- **AfriSpeech/african-speech-ipa** — type: dataset · Hugging Face · 2026-08-07 · tags: [asr, tts, corpus] · languages: [multilingual (African)]
  A new release from the AfriSpeech group pairing African-language text with IPA phonemic transcription, between 100k and 1M rows in parquet. The catalogue metadata carries a text modality tag and no audio tag, so despite the name this looks like a pronunciation lexicon or grapheme-to-phoneme resource rather than a speech corpus, which if true makes it more reusable than an audio release. The card was not reachable from this environment, so the language inventory, the licence, and above all whether the IPA was produced by human phonetic annotation or by an automatic G2P remain unverified.
  *Igbo relevance:* a pan-African IPA layer is the missing piece for an Igbo TTS front-end and for phoneme-based ASR that pools audio across languages, so the first check is whether Igbo appears at all and, if it does, whether the transcription encodes tone (two level tones plus downstep) or silently strips it, since an atonal IPA layer would be close to useless for Igbo synthesis. <https://huggingface.co/datasets/AfriSpeech/african-speech-ipa>

- **SilencioNetwork/igbo-speech** — type: dataset · Hugging Face · 2026-08-07 · tags: [asr, tts, corpus] · languages: [Igbo]
  A small crowdsourced monolingual Igbo speech corpus, fewer than 1,000 clips in audiofolder layout with paired transcripts, tagged for both ASR and TTS and released under CC-BY-NC-4.0. Both the audio and the annotations are marked crowdsourced, and a "human-transcription-on-demand" tag suggests collection and transcription run as an incremental commissioned pipeline rather than a finished fixed-size release, so the size may not be stable. At this scale and under a non-commercial licence it is an evaluation set or a fine-tuning increment, not a training corpus on its own.
  *Igbo relevance:* monolingual Igbo audio with human transcripts is scarce enough that even a sub-1k-clip set earns its place, most usefully as a held-out test set for the Nigerian-multilingual Whisper checkpoints already tracked here, where Igbo error rates are otherwise buried in a five-variety aggregate; before adopting it, verify whether the transcripts carry tone diacritics and how many distinct speakers and dialects are represented. <https://huggingface.co/datasets/SilencioNetwork/igbo-speech>

- **Development and Evaluation of HEAL, a Bilingual English-Luganda RAG Chatbot for Disease Surveillance in Uganda** — type: paper · Research Square · 2026-08-06 · tags: [llm, eval] · languages: [Luganda, English]
  A retrieval-augmented chatbot serving public-health disease-surveillance queries in both English and Luganda, from a large and largely Ugandan author group. OpenAlex has indexed no abstract for this preprint, so it is logged as a lead rather than a summarised result; the parts worth reading are how a retrieval corpus that is almost certainly English-only is made to answer in a low-resource Bantu language, and whether the evaluation measures Luganda generation quality directly or only scores the English path and assumes translation is lossless.
  *Igbo relevance:* an English knowledge base behind a local-language conversational interface is the most plausible near-term deployment shape for Igbo LLM work, and published African-language instances that include an evaluation component at all are rare, so this is worth reading specifically for how they assessed answer quality in a language with no reference QA set. <https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10357473/v1>

## 2026-08-07

- **Decolonizing Linguistic Policies in Automated Speech Recognition: A Framework for Cross-Culturally Competent Speech AI** — type: paper · arXiv · 2026-08-06 · tags: [asr, eval, method] · languages: [multilingual]
  Argues that the persistent failure of ASR on low-resource, Indigenous, and non-standard varieties is better read as de facto language policy than as a series of isolated engineering bugs, tracing how corpus composition, choice of metric, and model priors jointly decide which speakers a system can hear. The contribution is conceptual apparatus: a three-part harm taxonomy (misrecognition, misalignment, mistrust) and a seven-layer account of the situational factors, from phonology up to institutional setting, that govern who a voice interface serves. It closes with a participatory design protocol and a minimum audit checklist that places speaker communities as evaluators and governance partners rather than data sources. No systems are built or benchmarked, so it is a framework to apply, not evidence about any particular model.
  *Igbo relevance:* the minimum audit protocol is directly usable as a checklist for this project's ASR evaluation — it argues for per-dialect and per-accent error reporting with community sign-off on the test set, which is exactly what a single Nigerian-aggregate WER hides when Igbo contributes a small slice of the audio. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06141v1>

- **MameLoshnLM: Yiddish Language Model and Evaluation Benchmark** — type: model · arXiv · 2026-08-06 · tags: [llm, corpus, eval] · languages: [Yiddish]
  Continues pretraining Llama 3.1 8B on Oytser, a purpose-built Yiddish corpus combining web-native text with literary material, and evaluates it on Kashes, a multi-task benchmark spanning translation, linguistic analysis, information extraction, and language understanding. The diagnosis is more valuable than the checkpoint: the authors show that the Yiddish portions of existing multilingual corpora are heavily polluted with machine-translated and language-misclassified text, so scale on web data buys very little, and they demonstrate that their model's advantage over same-size open baselines concentrates in the lexical and morphological patterns that actually define the language rather than in aggregate scores.
  *Igbo relevance:* Igbo's slice of mC4/CC-100-style corpora suffers the same machine-translation contamination and language-ID misclassification, and this paper supplies both the audit that exposes it and a curate-then-continue-pretrain recipe at a scale one lab can afford — the contamination check is worth running on any Igbo web dump before it is used for pretraining. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05850v1>

- **Bangla Regional Dialect-to-Standard Bangla Parallel Corpus for Dialect Bias Evaluation** — type: dataset · Mendeley Data · 2026-08-05 · tags: [mt, corpus, eval] · languages: [Bangla]
  A four-way parallel corpus of 15,376 dialect-to-Standard-Bangla sentence pairs spanning Barishali, Chittagonian, Sylheti, and Noakhali, assembled by merging four existing releases and closing the Noakhali gap with 1,807 sentences translated by native speakers and independently checked. The transferable part is the splitting protocol: the 3,844 underlying meanings are assigned to train/dev/test first and only then expanded into dialect pairs, so no meaning straddles a split. The authors also report the diagnostic that undercuts their own benchmark — 99.8% of test targets still appear in training through a sibling dialect — which bounds how much a strong score on it can mean.
  *Igbo relevance:* kept for the evaluation design rather than the data, since Igbo dialect normalisation across Owerri, Onitsha, and Central Igbo hits precisely this leakage trap when the same meaning is elicited in several varieties; the meaning-first split and the sibling-overlap diagnostic are the two things to adopt before reporting any Igbo dialect-normalisation result. <https://doi.org/10.17632/42gvc83xkg>

- **IWAC Sentiment Analysis Visualization** — type: dataset · Zenodo · 2026-08-05 · tags: [sentiment, llm, eval, corpus] · languages: [French]
  Releases three-way LLM sentiment annotations over the Islam West Africa Collection — 12,287 francophone press articles from Benin, Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Niger, and Togo published between 1961 and 2025 — together with a SvelteKit dashboard for exploring them. GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3 Flash, and Ministral 3 14B each labelled every article on polarity, subjectivity, and how central Islam and Muslims are to the piece, and the interface surfaces per-model distributions alongside pairwise agreement including weighted Cohen's kappa, with geographic and temporal slices and CSV export. The design choice worth borrowing is the arbitration layer: rather than treating model disagreement as noise, blind judgements by a fourth model decide which annotation better fits the source text.
  *Igbo relevance:* Igbo sentiment work has no gold-annotated corpus at anything like this scale, and this is an inspectable end-to-end protocol for building one by multi-model annotation with kappa reporting and blind arbitration — the reported agreement levels also give a realistic prior for how far LLM labels on African-context text can be trusted before human adjudication is unavoidable. <https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21806224>

- **IWAC AI Pipelines** — type: dataset · Zenodo · 2026-08-05 · tags: [corpus, method, ner, asr, sentiment] · languages: [French]
  A code release (not a data or model release) of the Python pipelines behind the Islam West Africa Collection, wiring LLMs into archive curation end to end: OCR extraction and post-correction, summarisation, named-entity recognition with reconciliation against authority files, audio and video transcription, handwritten text recognition, and sentiment analysis, with every result written back into an Omeka S repository. It is engineering rather than research — no benchmark or ablation is reported — but it is one of the few published, runnable examples of the whole chain from scanned or recorded Africa-context material through to a queryable collection. The authority-reconciliation step in the NER stage is what separates it from a generic prompt-and-parse script.
  *Igbo relevance:* the Igbo digitisation backlog needs precisely this chain — OCR post-correction, ASR transcription, then NER over the output — so this supplies a working reference implementation and a storage pattern, letting effort go into the Igbo-specific pieces (diacritic-aware OCR correction, an Igbo gazetteer for the reconciliation step) instead of re-plumbing the pipeline. <https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21804211>

  A fine-tune of `openai/whisper-large-v3` released as a single checkpoint covering the five speech varieties that dominate Nigerian audio — Igbo, Yorùbá, Hausa, Nigerian English, and Nigerian Pidgin — rather than one model per language, which is the pragmatic choice when each language contributes only a few hours of transcribed speech. - **Large Language Models for Low-Resource Languages: A Conceptual Framework for an Electronic Explanatory Dictionary of the Tajik Language** — type: paper · arXiv · 2026-08-04 · tags: [llm, corpus, method] · languages: [Tajik]
  Proposes an architecture for building a monolingual explanatory dictionary with LLMs in the loop, decomposed into morphological analysis, lemmatisation, semantic clustering, and generation of the entries themselves, on top of a prior survey of what linguistic, statistical, and corpus resources for Tajik already exist. Two design commitments are argued rather than assumed: subword tokenisation chosen against the language's agglutinative morphology and high surface variability, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning as the adaptation strategy given almost no annotated data. It is explicitly a conceptual framework — no implementation, ablation, or evaluation is reported, so it offers a blueprint and a rationale, not evidence.
  *Igbo relevance:* Igbo has no machine-readable explanatory dictionary of comparable scope, and this module decomposition is a reusable plan for assembling one from existing scattered lexicographic material — kept as a borderline case, since the transfer is in the pipeline design and the morphology-driven tokenisation argument rather than in any validated result. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04186v1>

## 2026-08-04

- **Large language models for OCR in cultural heritage: a comparative study on Slovene Folkloristic texts** - type: paper · IJDAR (Springer) · 2026-08-01 · tags: [corpus, method, eval] · languages: [Slovene]
  Benchmarks six OCR routes - Tesseract alone, Tesseract with LLM post-correction, direct transcription by a general multimodal LLM, LLaMA 4 Maverick, Nanonets OCR-3, and Qwen-VL-OCR - against manually aligned ground truth on two Slovene heritage collections: uniformly typewritten folklore manuscripts and a visually heterogeneous children's magazine. Document-oriented and direct multimodal systems win on the clean typewritten material, while accuracy on the magazine tracks layout complexity more than model choice, so the authors argue for content-aware routing between strategies instead of one fixed pipeline. Scoring uses CER and WER plus sequence-based measures, which is the level of granularity needed to see whether errors are structural or character-level.
  *Igbo relevance:* the undigitised Igbo print archive - mission-era orthographies, dense sub-dot and tone diacritics, mixed layouts - is the same problem class, and this gives both a shortlist of OCR routes to try first and a warning that per-collection CER on diacritic-bearing vowels, not a single corpus-level number, is what decides whether the text is usable as training data. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10032-026-00612-7>

- **Seeking commonality while preserving diversity: A differential dual-path MoE solution for Multilingual Neural Machine Translation** - type: paper · Knowledge-Based Systems · 2026-08-01 · tags: [mt, method] · languages: [multilingual]
  Proposes a mixture-of-experts architecture for multilingual NMT built around two differentiated paths, one carrying structure shared across languages and one reserved for language-specific capacity, aimed squarely at the interference-versus-sharing tradeoff that governs many-to-many translation quality. OpenAlex has not indexed an abstract for this record, so it is logged as a lead: the routing scheme, expert budget, and language set still have to be read off the paper. It is tracked anyway because the shared/specific split is a core-task method question rather than a resource release.
  *Igbo relevance:* whether a low-resource language gains from or is drowned out by its high-resource neighbours in a massively multilingual model like NLLB-200 is decided by exactly this allocation, so the paper is a direct read on how to give Igbo dedicated capacity without losing cross-lingual transfer. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2026.116734>

- **Normal-Anchored First-Order Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning based Whisper Fine-Tuning for Enhancing Fairness of Cleft Lip and Palate Speech Recognition** - type: paper · arXiv · 2026-07-31 · tags: [asr, method] · languages: [unspecified]
  Adapts Whisper to cleft lip and palate speech with a first-order meta-learning scheme in which plentiful typical speech anchors the inner loop while severity-stratified pathological speech drives the outer loop, so the objective optimises for robustness after adaptation rather than fit to whichever condition dominates the training mix. The authors ablate which Whisper encoder layers to unfreeze (0–5, 4–11, 6–11, 8–11) alongside decoder and projection-head tuning on two clinical corpora, and report that anchoring on typical speech alone is not sufficient - severe cases still degrade badly, with word error rates above 50% where typical speech sits near 4%. The honest reporting of that residual gap is more useful than the headline averages.
  *Igbo relevance:* Igbo ASR faces the same data shape - one large anchor of abundant speech plus a small, internally heterogeneous target set spanning dialects and accents - so both the anchored meta-learning objective and the encoder-layer ablation are worth replicating directly when fine-tuning Whisper on the little transcribed Igbo audio that exists. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00186v1>

## 2026-08-04 (earlier batch)

- **matt-wisdom/KEmbed-naija-v3** (Hugging Face model, 2026-08-04) - A sentence-embedding model fine-tuned from BGE-M3 (an XLM-RoBERTa backbone) on roughly 57k Nigerian-context training pairs, trained with Matryoshka and multiple-negatives ranking objectives so the embeddings can be truncated to smaller dimensions without retraining. Because it targets Nigerian text rather than a single language, it is a candidate off-the-shelf retriever for mixed Igbo/English/Pidgin corpora, where most general multilingual encoders collapse Igbo into near-random vectors. Worth probing on Igbo-only retrieval before trusting it, since the training mix is not documented per-language. <https://huggingface.co/matt-wisdom/KEmbed-naija-v3>

- **Johniblazee/naija-speech-afrispeech-ng** (Hugging Face dataset, 2026-08-04) - A 10k–100k-example parquet release of paired audio and transcripts derived from the AfriSpeech-NG line of Nigerian accented-speech data, packaged for direct streaming through `datasets`, `dask`, or `polars`. For Igbo it is useful mainly as accent-adaptation and pretraining material: Igbo-accented Nigerian speech shifts the acoustic prior of ASR encoders even when the transcripts are English, which is the cheapest available lever before any Igbo-transcribed audio exists. The language and speaker breakdown needs auditing, as "Nigerian" collections routinely conflate several L1 backgrounds. <https://huggingface.co/datasets/Johniblazee/naija-speech-afrispeech-ng>

- **Axiveri/NaijaTTS-VITS-yo-mms-init** (Hugging Face model, 2026-08-03) - A VITS text-to-speech checkpoint for Yorùbá initialised from Meta's MMS weights rather than trained from scratch, with TensorBoard logs published alongside the model. The recipe is the interesting part for this project: Yorùbá and Igbo are both tonal Nigerian languages with diacritic-bearing orthographies, so an MMS-initialised VITS pipeline is close to a drop-in template for Igbo TTS, including the question of whether tone marks survive the text frontend. Download counts are currently zero, so treat it as a reference implementation rather than a validated system. <https://huggingface.co/Axiveri/NaijaTTS-VITS-yo-mms-init>

- **Domain-Specific Evaluation of Text-to-Speech Systems: A Multi-Metric Benchmarking Study** (arXiv, 2026-08-03) - Proposes a reproducible TTS evaluation harness that combines listener studies (MUSHRA, ABX) with objective measures - speaker similarity via Resemblyzer, mel-cepstral distortion, and F0 RMSE - and applies it to four systems (Indic-Parler-TTS, MMS-TTS, Edge TTS, Gemini TTS) across formal, conversational, literary, and emotional speech in one low-resource language. The headline finding is that synthesis quality swings sharply by domain, with emotional speech worst, so single-number MOS comparisons hide most of what matters. The released scripts and notebooks are directly reusable for Igbo, and the F0 RMSE component is especially apt for a tonal language where pitch errors are lexical errors, not merely prosodic ones. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02235v1>

- **Xhenifer/sesotho-sentiment-afriberta** (Hugging Face model, 2026-08-03) - A Sesotho sentiment classifier fine-tuned from AfriBERTa-large, tagged for cross-orthography robustness and released under CC-BY-NC-4.0. Its relevance to Igbo runs through the backbone: AfriBERTa is one of the few pretrained encoders whose training corpus actually includes Igbo, so this is a worked example of how much downstream classification signal a small African-language fine-tune can pull out of it. The cross-orthography framing also maps onto the practical Igbo problem of text appearing with, without, or with inconsistent diacritics. <https://huggingface.co/Xhenifer/sesotho-sentiment-afriberta>

- **Anthropological Linguistic Study of Names as Representations of Family and Community Origins and Identity Among the Igbo of Southeastern Nigeria** (Genealogy, 2026-08-03) - Analyses how Igbo family and community names encode ancestry, kinship, spatial location, and ritual role, using anthropological and Cultural Linguistics frameworks, and argues that such names act as compressed historical records rather than bare identifiers. Along the way it documents recurring compositional patterns - `umu` plus a progenitor's name, or a name plus a numeral marker - which are morphological facts and not only cultural ones. That makes it useful groundwork for Igbo named-entity recognition and gazetteer construction, where multi-token community and family names are systematically mishandled by taggers trained on English-style person names. <https://doi.org/10.3390/genealogy10030100>

- **Latent Softmax for Data-Efficient Phoneme-Based Multilingual ASR Across Tonal and Non-Tonal Languages** (arXiv, 2026-08-02) - Addresses a supervision mismatch in joint tonal/non-tonal phoneme ASR: tonal corpora label tone-marked vowels while non-tonal corpora label only base vowels, so a flat softmax either severs the two label sets or discards tone. The proposed CTC-compatible output layer treats tone-marked vowels as subclasses under base-vowel major classes and marginalises the unobserved subclass when only a base-vowel label is available, cutting phoneme error rates by 8–18% on Mandarin and English benchmarks and improving downstream phoneme-to-grapheme decoding. This is the most directly applicable item in today's batch for Igbo: it is a principled way to pool English audio with the small amount of tone-marked Igbo transcription that exists, without forcing a choice between cross-lingual sharing and tonal fidelity. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01281v1>

- **Low-Resource Machine Translation of Yoruba Text to Nigerian Pidgin Using NLLB-200 and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning** (Cureus Journal of Computer Science, 2026-08-03) - Adapts NLLB-200 (600M) to Yorùbá→Nigerian Pidgin using LoRA on the attention layers over a hand-curated corpus of just 1,846 sentence pairs, with an ablation over LoRA rank and beam width. The zero-shot baseline is essentially broken (BLEU 0.74) and the tuned model reaches BLEU 30.97 with chrF 50.63, which is the paper's real contribution: a demonstration of how far parameter-efficient adaptation can go from a corpus small enough for one team to build by hand. The setup transfers almost directly to Igbo - a tonal Nigerian language paired with a contact language that has no settled orthography - and it sets a realistic expectation for what a few thousand curated Igbo pairs should buy. <https://doi.org/10.7759/s44389-026-00202-y>

- **Enhancing Myanmar Sign Language Translation Through Transfer Learning, Self-training, and Error Correction** (ACM TALLIP, 2026-08-03) - Fine-tunes mT5 and mBART-50 for translation between Myanmar Sign Language glosses and written Myanmar, then stacks three techniques against extreme data scarcity: pretraining on a large ASL–English parallel corpus before re-training on the tiny in-domain corpus, self-training by translating monolingual target text into synthetic pairs, and a separate mT5 error-correction model trained on artificially perturbed sentences. Each component contributes measurably, and the paper also compares four token-segmentation schemes. The language pair is far from Igbo, but the pipeline is the one Igbo MT actually needs - pivot from a high-resource pair, amplify a few thousand gold pairs with monolingual text, then clean up grammatical damage in a separate pass. <https://doi.org/10.1145/3837853>

- **English Influence on the Structure of Igbo Personal Names** (EDUMALSYS Journal of Research in Education Management, 2026-08-02) - A qualitative contact-linguistics study of 68 Igbo personal names, cataloguing the phonological changes English contact induces (vowel substitution, stress shift, loss of tone) alongside morphological ones (truncation, respelling, gemination, English–Igbo hybrid forms). For Igbo NLP this reads as a specification of the noise model that real-world text exhibits: the documented tone loss and respelling patterns are exactly the variation that breaks exact-match entity linking and makes diacritic restoration necessary rather than optional. The catalogue of transformation types is concrete enough to seed normalisation rules or synthetic-variant generation for NER training data. <https://doi.org/10.58578/edumalsys.v4i3.11635>

- **Digital Documentation of Igbo Cultural Heritage and its Preservation in a Pluralistic Nigerian Society** (Zenodo, 2026-08-02) - A descriptive, secondary-source survey arguing that Igbo language material, folklore, proverbs, oral history, and religious texts remain largely undigitised, and reviewing the tooling and funding obstacles to changing that. It is a position paper rather than a technical contribution, and its cost and expertise estimates are informal. It is logged here because it maps the offline paper-based Igbo corpus that any serious Igbo LLM or MT effort will eventually need to source from, and it names the institutional actors and document categories involved - useful when planning corpus acquisition and provenance documentation. <https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21755899>

- **Axiveri/naija-tts-pcm-processed** (Hugging Face dataset, 2026-08-01) - A preprocessed Nigerian Pidgin speech corpus of 10k–100k audio/text pairs in audiofolder layout, evidently the training data behind the same author's NaijaTTS VITS checkpoints. Its value for Igbo is as a paired example of the whole loop - a small Nigerian-language TTS corpus published alongside the model trained on it - which is the shape an Igbo TTS release would take, and the segmentation and normalisation choices baked into the "processed" version are worth inspecting before rebuilding that pipeline. Nigerian Pidgin is not tonal in the way Igbo is, so the text frontend will not carry over unchanged. <https://huggingface.co/datasets/Axiveri/naija-tts-pcm-processed>

- **Contrastive Preference Optimization for Low-Resource Vietnamese to Khmer Neural Machine Translation** (Communications in Computer and Information Science, 2026-08-02) - Applies contrastive preference optimization - a preference-based training objective that teaches a translation model to prefer better outputs over merely adequate ones, rather than only maximising likelihood on references - to an extremely low-resource Vietnamese→Khmer pair. OpenAlex has not indexed an abstract for this record, so it is logged as a lead rather than a summarised result; the specific data conditions and gains still need to be read off the paper. It is tracked because preference-based objectives are one of the few levers that improve low-resource MT without new parallel data, which is precisely the constraint on Igbo↔English translation. <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-92-2590-3_22>

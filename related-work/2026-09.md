# Related work - September 2026

## 2026-09-03

- **SSPA: Enhancing Pseudo-Corpus Quality on Tibetan Machine Translation via Semantic-Syntax Prealignment** - type: paper · Computers (MDPI) · 2026-09-02 · tags: [mt, corpus, method] · languages: [Tibetan, English]
  Proposes Semantic-Syntax Prealignment (SSPA), a pseudo-parallel corpus generation framework for Tibetan-English MT that standardizes source-target structural representations through length-unified truncation and terminology normalization, then maximizes syntactic-semantic similarity to professional reference texts before accepting synthetic pairs. This targets the stylistic mismatch and agglutinative-morphology violations that plague naively back-translated pseudo-corpora under extreme data scarcity.
  *Igbo relevance:* the prealignment recipe for filtering and shaping pseudo-parallel data by syntactic-semantic closeness to professional references is directly reusable for improving synthetic Igbo-English training pairs built from noisy back-translation or scraped sources. <https://doi.org/10.3390/computers15090576>

- **SpeakPay: Domain-Adaptive LoRA Fine-Tuning of Whisper for Low-Resource Nepali Financial Speech Recognition** - type: paper · arXiv · 2026-09-01 · tags: [asr, method] · languages: [Nepali]
  Introduces NepFinSpeech-403, a 403-utterance dataset of Nepali financial voice commands spanning 237 unique numerals, and fine-tunes Whisper large-v2 with LoRA for domain adaptation. Adaptation cuts WER from 129.95% (zero-shot) to 42.58% and raises Devanagari numeral recognition accuracy from 0% to 73.9%, while showing that word-level metrics understate real task accuracy for numeral-heavy commands.
  *Igbo relevance:* shows a small (~400-utterance) domain-specific set plus LoRA fine-tuning can turn a near-unusable zero-shot Whisper baseline into a workable system, a low-cost recipe applicable to Igbo voice-command or financial-domain ASR. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01737v1>

- **Real-time error correction model for spoken English translation combining Mistral and self-supervised learning** - type: paper · Discover Artificial Intelligence · 2026-09-01 · tags: [asr, mt, method] · languages: [English, unspecified source]
  Proposes a real-time error-correction model for spoken translation pairing Mistral with self-supervised learning, using parallel decoding and hierarchical noise simulation to cut inference latency (180ms to 50ms) and raise technical-term correction accuracy. With only 1% labeled data, the self-supervised setup reaches BLEU 25 versus a baseline of 12.
  *Igbo relevance:* the noise-simulation-based self-supervised pretraining strategy for coping with severe label scarcity is a plausible template for bootstrapping Igbo speech-translation or ASR error-correction models where transcribed data is scarce; the abstract does not specify the source language, so the transfer should be checked against the full paper. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-02072-8>

- **g-vamp: Improving sequence labelling frameworks for multiword expressions identification** - type: paper · Array (Elsevier) · 2026-09-01 · tags: [method] · languages: [multilingual, PARSEME collection]
  Introduces g-vamp (Generate Variants and Merge Predictions), a framework letting sequence-labelling multiword-expression identifiers handle token sharing, where a single token belongs to more than one expression, without discarding overlapping annotations as most current systems do to resolve the conflict. This avoids the systematic information loss and prediction errors that token-sharing conflicts otherwise cause, particularly in low-resource settings.
  *Igbo relevance:* Igbo has many multiword and compound expressions; this token-sharing-aware sequence-labelling framework is directly applicable to building an Igbo multiword-expression or NER tagger without the usual overlap-discarding corpus damage. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.array.2026.101164>

- **Low-resource hallucination detection in LLMs on multi-task datasets via iterative pseudo-labeling using confidence thresholding and active learning** - type: paper · Discover Artificial Intelligence · 2026-09-01 · tags: [eval, llm, mt] · languages: [English, unspecified]
  Develops an iterative self-training framework for detecting LLM hallucinations across machine translation, definition modeling, and paraphrase generation using the SemEval-2024 SHROOM dataset, expanding a small labeled seed set via confidence-thresholded pseudo-labeling and active learning rather than collecting more annotations.
  *Igbo relevance:* the confidence-thresholded pseudo-labeling plus active-learning loop is a direct fit for building Igbo hallucination or MT-quality classifiers where only a handful of manually labeled examples exist and further annotation is expensive. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-02106-1>

- **Quality Estimation for Machine Translation in Low-Resource Settings** - type: paper (PhD thesis) · University of Surrey Open Research repository · 2026-09-01 · tags: [mt, eval] · languages: [nine low-resource pairs, unspecified list]
  A thesis investigating MT quality estimation, predicting translation quality without reference translations, across nine low-resource language pairs, covering multilingual modelling, architectural adaptations, data curation, and efficient learning strategies. Multilingual QE training on linguistically related languages consistently beats single-pair training, and LLMs used zero-shot, in-context, or fine-tuned for QE generalize broadly but show concrete limitations on low-resource languages.
  *Igbo relevance:* directly actionable for Igbo MT evaluation, grouping Igbo with linguistically related Niger-Congo languages during QE training rather than training a single-pair model is shown here to help, and the documented LLM-QE limitations flag what to check before trusting an LLM-as-judge for Igbo translation quality. <https://doi.org/10.15126/thesis.902178>

- **Neuromagnetic representation of vowel prosody in the auditory cortex** - type: paper · Scientific Reports · 2026-09-01 · tags: [asr, tts] · languages: [unspecified, tone-perception psycholinguistics]
  An MEG study finding that falling pitch contours in vowels elicit larger, earlier P2 auditory-cortex responses than rising or flat contours, and that varying pitch contour across a vowel sequence itself boosts the P2 response, suggesting human audition is asymmetrically and categorically sensitive to particular pitch-glide shapes.
  *Igbo relevance:* since Igbo tone contrasts are carried by comparable pitch-contour shapes, this asymmetric perceptual sensitivity (falling contours more salient than rising ones) is a testable hypothesis for prioritising which tone contrasts most need explicit modelling in Igbo TTS prosody or tone-annotation schemes. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-68466-x>

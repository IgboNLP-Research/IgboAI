# Related work - September 2026

## 2026-09-04

- **Translation as a Decision Space: A Multi-Agent Perspective on Low-Resource Dialect Generation** - type: paper · arXiv · 2026-09-03 · tags: [mt, method] · languages: [Turkish, Syrian Arabic]
  Reframes low-resource dialect MT as a decision space explored by three agents over a shared multilingual backbone: zero-shot direct translation, a lightweight LoRA-style dialect-stabilization fine-tune, and English-pivot translation, tested on Turkish-to-Syrian-Arabic dialogue. Rather than chasing a single best output, the authors treat inter-agent divergence as an interpretability signal, showing that stabilization nearly doubles dialect-marker frequency while pivoting compresses output toward standardized forms and zero-shot decoding shows the widest behavioral variance.
  *Igbo relevance:* the dialect-stabilization-via-lightweight-fine-tuning recipe and the multi-agent divergence framing are directly applicable to Igbo, whose major dialects (Owerri, Onitsha, Waawa, Bonny) and heavy English contact create exactly the kind of decision-space ambiguity this method is designed to expose and control. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04048v1>

- **Artificial Intelligent (AI) and Endangered Language Preservation: Challenges Among Igbo, Hausa and Yoruba** - type: paper · Zenodo · 2026-09-02 · tags: [eval] · languages: [Igbo, Hausa, Yoruba]
  A cross-sectional questionnaire study of 45 married Igbo, Hausa, and Yoruba adults in Nigeria, using descriptive statistics to characterize which AI tools this community reports using for language documentation and what technical and ethical obstacles they perceive. The paper is a perception survey rather than a technical contribution, but it argues that AI could capture tonal and idiomatic nuance in these languages given adequate computational resources.
  *Igbo relevance:* surfaces community-reported adoption barriers and awareness gaps for AI-driven Igbo language tools, useful context when designing user-facing Igbo NLP systems, though the finding rests on a small (n=45), non-technical perception survey. <https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22246073>

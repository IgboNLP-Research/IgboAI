# Related Work

## 2026-08-04

- **matt-wisdom/KEmbed-naija-v3** (Hugging Face model, 2026-08-04) — A sentence-embedding model fine-tuned from BGE-M3 (an XLM-RoBERTa backbone) on roughly 57k Nigerian-context training pairs, trained with Matryoshka and multiple-negatives ranking objectives so the embeddings can be truncated to smaller dimensions without retraining. Because it targets Nigerian text rather than a single language, it is a candidate off-the-shelf retriever for mixed Igbo/English/Pidgin corpora, where most general multilingual encoders collapse Igbo into near-random vectors. Worth probing on Igbo-only retrieval before trusting it, since the training mix is not documented per-language. <https://huggingface.co/matt-wisdom/KEmbed-naija-v3>

- **Johniblazee/naija-speech-afrispeech-ng** (Hugging Face dataset, 2026-08-04) — A 10k–100k-example parquet release of paired audio and transcripts derived from the AfriSpeech-NG line of Nigerian accented-speech data, packaged for direct streaming through `datasets`, `dask`, or `polars`. For Igbo it is useful mainly as accent-adaptation and pretraining material: Igbo-accented Nigerian speech shifts the acoustic prior of ASR encoders even when the transcripts are English, which is the cheapest available lever before any Igbo-transcribed audio exists. The language and speaker breakdown needs auditing, as "Nigerian" collections routinely conflate several L1 backgrounds. <https://huggingface.co/datasets/Johniblazee/naija-speech-afrispeech-ng>

- **Axiveri/NaijaTTS-VITS-yo-mms-init** (Hugging Face model, 2026-08-03) — A VITS text-to-speech checkpoint for Yorùbá initialised from Meta's MMS weights rather than trained from scratch, with TensorBoard logs published alongside the model. The recipe is the interesting part for this project: Yorùbá and Igbo are both tonal Nigerian languages with diacritic-bearing orthographies, so an MMS-initialised VITS pipeline is close to a drop-in template for Igbo TTS, including the question of whether tone marks survive the text frontend. Download counts are currently zero, so treat it as a reference implementation rather than a validated system. <https://huggingface.co/Axiveri/NaijaTTS-VITS-yo-mms-init>

- **Domain-Specific Evaluation of Text-to-Speech Systems: A Multi-Metric Benchmarking Study** (arXiv, 2026-08-03) — Proposes a reproducible TTS evaluation harness that combines listener studies (MUSHRA, ABX) with objective measures — speaker similarity via Resemblyzer, mel-cepstral distortion, and F0 RMSE — and applies it to four systems (Indic-Parler-TTS, MMS-TTS, Edge TTS, Gemini TTS) across formal, conversational, literary, and emotional speech in one low-resource language. The headline finding is that synthesis quality swings sharply by domain, with emotional speech worst, so single-number MOS comparisons hide most of what matters. The released scripts and notebooks are directly reusable for Igbo, and the F0 RMSE component is especially apt for a tonal language where pitch errors are lexical errors, not merely prosodic ones. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02235v1>

- **Xhenifer/sesotho-sentiment-afriberta** (Hugging Face model, 2026-08-03) — A Sesotho sentiment classifier fine-tuned from AfriBERTa-large, tagged for cross-orthography robustness and released under CC-BY-NC-4.0. Its relevance to Igbo runs through the backbone: AfriBERTa is one of the few pretrained encoders whose training corpus actually includes Igbo, so this is a worked example of how much downstream classification signal a small African-language fine-tune can pull out of it. The cross-orthography framing also maps onto the practical Igbo problem of text appearing with, without, or with inconsistent diacritics. <https://huggingface.co/Xhenifer/sesotho-sentiment-afriberta>

- **Anthropological Linguistic Study of Names as Representations of Family and Community Origins and Identity Among the Igbo of Southeastern Nigeria** (Genealogy, 2026-08-03) — Analyses how Igbo family and community names encode ancestry, kinship, spatial location, and ritual role, using anthropological and Cultural Linguistics frameworks, and argues that such names act as compressed historical records rather than bare identifiers. Along the way it documents recurring compositional patterns — `umu` plus a progenitor's name, or a name plus a numeral marker — which are morphological facts and not only cultural ones. That makes it useful groundwork for Igbo named-entity recognition and gazetteer construction, where multi-token community and family names are systematically mishandled by taggers trained on English-style person names. <https://doi.org/10.3390/genealogy10030100>
